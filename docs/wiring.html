Tip = supply, ring = signal, sleeve = ground, on every jack; pin 1 unused. Supplies on the tip make hot-plugging safe, and a plug in the wrong 3.5 mm jack does almost no harm: a valve in a transducer jack stays shut, and an O2 cell in a valve jack has no circuit because the valve jack's sleeve is left unconnected. Avoid a transducer in a valve jack: its supply pin sees 12 V, with a return through its output pin whenever that valve is driven. The compressor sense uses a 2.5 mm jack so no other plug can fake "compressor running". A 2.5 mm plug with ring and sleeve joined does fake it on purpose: bench tests only, labelled TEST ONLY.

Before any gas is connected

With the 12 V supply off and the board powered from USB, flash it and confirm each valve board's PWM pin stays below 0.5 V throughout. That proves the pull-downs are holding the valves shut through reset and flashing.
  • Fit the flyback diode on both valves; without it the turn-off spike eventually kills the MOSFET.
  • Use a regulated 12 V supply and measure it under load. The firmware caps valve drive at 80% (VALVE_MAX_DUTY, 9.6 V average) so the 10 V coils stay within 1.8 W; a supply above 12 V overdrives them.
  • Set the O2 and He regulators below the valves' 100 psig rating.
  • Keep the SD slot empty: GPIO 11 and 12 are its data and clock lines.
  • Clamp the current switch around one conductor only; around the whole cable the currents cancel. Work on the mains side belongs with someone qualified.
  • Calibrate both O2 cells in air from the unit before blending. The valves stay closed until the cells have been calibrated.
